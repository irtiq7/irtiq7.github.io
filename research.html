Research

My research lies at the intersection of statistical signal processing, robot audition, and autonomous systems. My doctoral thesis, Acoustic Echo Estimation using the model-based approach with Application to Spatial Map Construction in Robotics, was completed at Aalborg University in 2021.

  • Acoustic echo estimation

    Estimating times of arrival and directions of arrival of acoustic echoes with expectation-maximization methods, including settings with multiple microphones and the robot's own ego-noise.

    Related publications →

  • Reflector localization and spatial maps

    Using echoes to localize acoustic reflectors and construct spatial maps for robotic platforms, including sound-based distance estimation for indoor navigation.

  • Machine learning for acoustic mapping

    Learning-based frameworks for acoustic reflector mapping, building on the model-based approach.

  • Robot audition

    An overview of how robots can perceive the world with sound is given in the chapter Perceiving the World With Sound.
